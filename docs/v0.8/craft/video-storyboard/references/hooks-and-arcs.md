# Opening hooks and narrative arcs

Opening hooks are the first one to three seconds of a video, designed to stop a scrolling viewer. Narrative arcs are
the shapes a short film's story can take. Pick the arc first, then the hook that opens it.

## What a hook needs in the first seconds of a short vertical ad

The first one or two seconds of a short vertical ad are the hook. What a hook needs is three things within about
1.5 s: a pattern interrupt (the frame breaks the feed's look), an emotional charge (surprise, curiosity,
aspiration), and an open loop (a question the viewer wants closed). Frame 0 is the thumbnail on most players, so the
hook's first frame must already show something.

## Hook budget by platform

Hook budget by platform, as rough guides:

| Platform | Sound on by default | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| Short vertical feeds (Reels, Shorts, TikTok) | often | under 1 s |
| Feed video on professional networks | usually off | about 1.2 s; captions carry the hook |
| Image-first feeds | off | about 1 s |
| Long-form video sites | on | about 3 s |
| Website hero loop | ambient | about 4 s |
| Event screen | on, captive | none needed |

## Seven hook patterns

Seven hook patterns and how to build each:

| Pattern | Build | Copy shape | Kills it |
|---|---|---|---|
| The number | a huge figure lands at 0.2 to 0.4 s, holds, then one line of context | "$2.4B" then "processed in 12 months" | explaining the number in the same frame |
| The question | one short question, word by word, then a beat of silence | "What if the video wrote itself?" | answering at once |
| The contrast | split frame: before against after, or old against new | "Before / After" | a slow wipe that hides the contrast |
| The impact | a hard hit of sound and picture on frame 0 to 0.3 s | a single word | a soft fade-in |
| Whisper to roar | a small, quiet detail that cuts to full scale | "it starts with one line" | staying small too long |
| The demo | the product doing the thing in the first second | none needed | a logo before the demo |
| Social proof | a quick flash of a recognisable endorsement or metric | "Used by 40,000 teams" | unverifiable claims |

## Seven narrative arcs

Seven narrative arcs with typical lengths:

| Arc | Length | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Impact reveal | 8 to 20 s | tease, build, reveal, hold the logo |
| Problem to solution | 15 to 45 s | pain, agitation, turn, solution, proof, call to action |
| Feature journey | 20 to 60 s | promise, three to five features with one hold each, recap |
| Data story | 20 to 45 s | context number, the change, the reason, the takeaway |
| Brand manifesto | 20 to 60 s | belief, tension, image sequence, resolve on the line |
| Social hook | 6 to 15 s | hook, one payoff, loopable end |
| Tutorial | 30 to 120 s | outcome first, steps with holds, recap |

Each arc has one peak. Put the boldest motion and transition there and let the beats before and after it breathe.

## Choosing an arc

Choosing an arc follows the brief:

- One big announcement: impact reveal.
- A pain the audience knows: problem to solution.
- Several capabilities: feature journey.
- A metric is the story: data story.
- Values or a launch film: brand manifesto.
- A vertical feed post: social hook.
- Teaching a task: tutorial.

When the brief is open, describe three storylines, each a different film, in words only, and let the person
pick before building.
